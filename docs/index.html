01 / Project brief

Built to demonstrate product thinking, not just CRUD.

The project models the decisions a real HR system needs: separated identities, explicit access rules, protected workflows, relational integrity, and testable business constraints.

Problem

HR data is connected

Employees, departments, attendance, leave, and salary records cannot be treated as isolated forms. Their relationships and lifecycle rules matter.

Approach

One governed workflow

Custom RBAC, permission middleware, Form Requests, services, and Eloquent relationships keep each operation authorized and predictable.

Outcome

A portfolio-grade system

Ten connected product areas, read-only reports, documented architecture, and an automated suite covering the essential HR workflows.

02 / Architecture

A clear path from request to business result.

Responsibilities stay visible and narrow. Laravel conventions handle the request lifecycle while custom domain rules live where they can be reused and tested.

Layer 01

Route + middleware

Session authentication and module.action permission checks guard every protected entry point.

Layer 02

Form Request

Validation and request authorization stay outside controllers and close to the input boundary.

Layer 03

Thin controller

Controllers coordinate dependencies, responses, and redirects without owning domain logic.

Layer 04

Service + model

Business rules and Eloquent relationships manage protected operations and persistence.

Layer 05

Blade response

The permission-aware UI exposes only the navigation and actions available to the current user.

Key decision

The system uses Laravel's session guard with custom Blade authentication and custom RBAC—without Breeze, Jetstream, Spatie Permission, Livewire, React, or Vue. This keeps the authorization model explicit and demonstrates the underlying Laravel mechanics.

03 / Product scope

The complete HR workflow, split into focused modules.

Each area has its own routes, permissions, validation, relationships, and UI while remaining part of one coherent operational system.

01

Authentication

Custom login and POST-only logout, session regeneration, CSRF protection, and inactive-user checks.

02

Roles & permissions

Many-to-many RBAC with system-defined permissions, role assignment, and Super Admin safeguards.

03

Departments

Organizational units with active status, soft deletion, and protection for related records.

04

Designations

Department-linked job titles with code-based records and relationship-aware validation.

05

Employees

HR identities connected to users, departments, designations, status, and employment dates.

06

Attendance

Daily check-in/out records with status tracking and duplicate employee-date prevention.

07

Leave

Leave types, balances, requests, approval/rejection flow, and overlapping-date protection.

08

Payroll

Salary structures, payroll runs, itemized payslips, net salary calculation, and period integrity.

09

Dashboard

Operational totals, recent activity, and quick links filtered by the current user's permissions.

10

Reports

Read-only employee, attendance, leave, and payroll views with validated filters.

04 / Data model

Business identity stays separate from login identity.

The schema groups access control, organization, attendance, leave, and payroll into connected business contexts instead of one oversized employee table.

17

Core business tables

Including RBAC pivots and itemized payroll tables. Laravel infrastructure tables are intentionally excluded from the project ERD.
